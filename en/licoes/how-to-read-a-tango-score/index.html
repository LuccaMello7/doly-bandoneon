How to read a tango score

Scores for solo bandoneon usually use two staves (treble for the right hand, bass for the left), like the piano. In orquesta típica parts, the bandoneon often has a single staff in treble clef, with the melody or a countermelody. The basses go to the double bass and the piano.

What to look for when you open a tango:

  • Form: traditional tangos usually have two sections (A and B), and older ones sometimes a third (trio). Find where each section starts.
  • Repeats: repeat bars, D.C. (back to the beginning), D.S. (back to the sign 𝄋), Coda (𝄌) and Fine (end).
  • Key and time signature: many tangos are in a minor key and in 2/4 or 4/4.
  • Expression marks: marcato, legato, rall. (slowing down), a tempo, and dynamics such as p, f and sfz.

Tip: with a digital score (MuseScore), export to MusicXML and use Melodies → Import MusicXML to practice it section by section. Prefer public-domain works or scores you have the right to use.
