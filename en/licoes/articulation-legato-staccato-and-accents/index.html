Articulation: legato, staccato and accents

On the bandoneon, articulation comes from combining fingers and bellows:

  • Legato (connected): one note flows into the next with no silence. The bellows keeps moving and the fingers change buttons at exactly the right moment. It is easier when the notes of the phrase are in the same bellows direction.
  • Staccato (detached): short notes with silence between them. The finger releases the button quickly; the bellows can keep moving smoothly.
  • Accent: a quick push of the bellows at the start of the note (or chord) makes it louder. This is what gives marcato its weight.
  • Arrastre: the sound starts a little before the beat and grows into the accent. It comes from a bellows movement that speeds up toward the strong beat.

Try it on the Keyboard tab: play a scale legato and then staccato. On the Rhythm tab, listen to the "Marcato with arrastre" pattern.
