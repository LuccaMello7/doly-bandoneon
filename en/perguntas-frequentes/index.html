Frequently asked questions

Everything people usually ask about the bandoneon, about how to start studying and about BanDoly.

The instrument

What is the bandoneon?#

It is the bellows-and-buttons instrument that gives tango its voice. It was created in Germany in the 19th century and became the symbol of the tango of Buenos Aires and Montevideo. It is bisonoric: each button plays one note when opening the bellows and another when closing.

What is the difference between the bandoneon and the accordion?#

Both have bellows, but they work differently. The accordion usually has a keyboard or buttons for the right hand and, on the left, ready-made basses and chords: one button plays a whole chord. On the bandoneon, both hands play single notes, and chords are built note by note. It is also held differently: resting on the legs, with both boxes strapped to the hands.

How many buttons does a bandoneon have?#

The one most used in tango, the Rheinische 142, has 71 buttons: 38 for the right hand and 33 for the left. Since each button gives one note when the bellows open and another when they close, that makes 142 voices, which is where the name comes from. There are also models with a different number of voices.

What does bisonoric mean?#

That the same button gives two notes: one when you open the bellows and another when you close them. That is why bandoneon players learn four button maps: right hand opening, right hand closing, left hand opening and left hand closing.

Is the bandoneon hard to learn?#

It has a reputation for being hard because the buttons do not follow the order of the notes and each button changes note with the bellows. But the difficulty is mostly memory, and memory can be trained: with short daily practice, the button map becomes natural. That is what the BanDoly challenges and Academy are for.

What bandoneon systems are there?#

Tango almost always uses the Rheinische (Rhineland) system, which is bisonoric, like the 142-voice model. There are others, such as the German Einheitsbandoneon and chromatic bandoneons, such as the Péguri system, which give the same note opening and closing. Before studying from a method or buying an instrument, check which system it uses.

Getting started

Do I need to read sheet music?#

Not to get started. In BanDoly you can play by following the highlighted buttons and the Falling notes mode, where the notes fall to a line in time with the music. Little by little, the lessons and the Staff reading challenge teach you to read the two clefs used on the bandoneon.

How long does it take to learn to play?#

It depends on how much and how you practise. With about 20 to 30 minutes a day, many people play simple melodies within a few weeks. Playing tango with both hands and with expression takes years of study, as with any instrument.

Where do I start?#

On your first visit, BanDoly asks three questions and builds your study path. It lives in Learn → My study, with the next step always at hand. If you prefer to go on your own, start with lessons 1 to 4 and then play freely on the Keyboard.

How long should I practise each day?#

A little every day pays off more than a lot now and then. A good routine takes 20 to 30 minutes: bellows, the button map, rhythm and repertoire. Today’s routine, in My study, suggests five short tasks and counts the days in a row.

Do I need a teacher?#

Whenever you can, yes. BanDoly supports your studies and will never replace a teacher: a teacher corrects your posture, bellows technique and fingering, things an app cannot see.

Owning a bandoneon

Do I need a bandoneon to use BanDoly?#

No. You can learn the button map, reading and rhythms with the virtual keyboard alone, on a computer or a phone. If you have a bandoneon, the app can also listen to you play through the microphone and check the notes.

Which bandoneon should I buy to start?#

To play tango, look for a Rheinische-system bandoneon, ideally with 142 voices, which is the map BanDoly uses. Many instruments for sale are old: before buying, ask a bandoneon player or a technician to check the bellows, the reeds, the tuning and that every note works.

Does the app work with my real bandoneon?#

Yes. In Play → Real bandoneon, the app listens through the microphone, shows the note and its tuning and lights up the button that gives that note. In the Academy and in Melodies, it also checks the exercises through the microphone.

My bandoneon has different notes from the app. What now?#

Instruments of the same system sometimes have small differences. In Keyboard → Button map → Edit layout, you can change the notes of any button to match your instrument.

About BanDoly

Is BanDoly free?#

Yes. BanDoly is free and needs no sign-up. Your progress is saved in your browser, and you can export a backup in Settings.

Does it work on phones and offline?#

Yes. The keyboard adapts to the phone screen and accepts several fingers at once to play chords. If you install the app with the button in Settings, it works even offline.

Which languages is BanDoly available in?#

In Portuguese, Spanish and English. On your first visit, the app suggests your browser’s language, and you can switch at any time with PT, ES and EN at the top of the screen.

Is my progress saved?#

It is saved in your own browser, with no sign-up. So you lose nothing when you clear the browser, or to take everything to another device, use the backup in Profile → Settings.

Does BanDoly collect data about me?#

We do not collect personal data and we do not use cookies. To know how many people use the app, we count visits with GoatCounter, which records only general figures, such as the page opened, the country and the type of device, without identifying anyone. Your progress never leaves your browser.

Why the name BanDoly?#

It is a tribute to Doly Costa, a bandoneon player from Caçapava do Sul in southern Brazil who, at 97, still plays: bandoneon + Doly. Read his story.
