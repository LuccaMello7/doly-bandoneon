Intervals

An interval is the distance between two notes. It has a number and a quality, and each comes from counting:

  1. Count the notes, including the first and the last: C, D, E are three, so C–E is a third.
  2. Count the semitones: from C to E there are 4, so the third is major. With 3 semitones (C–E♭) it would be minor.

Fourths, fifths and octaves can be perfect; seconds, thirds, sixths and sevenths are major or minor. The augmented fourth, with 6 semitones, is the tritone.

Why it matters on the bandoneon

Because each button plays one note opening and another closing, the same interval has different shapes in the two bellows directions. On the right hand, opening, there are 34 major thirds in 32 different shapes. That is why it pays to learn intervals by sound and by counting, not just by finger position.
